01 / The place and the question

The quarry
keeps
other times.

The sea has withdrawn. The machines have fallen quiet. The ground is still being written.
What begins to speak when I stop looking for a landscape and start listening to what it leaves behind?
A field note from ENCI

I came looking for a landscape and found a multitude of unfinished stories. A square cut into concrete. A thin green life at the edge of a rusted plate. A cliff made from an ancient sea. Objects that did not ask to become art, only to be noticed.

Here, nature is not simply taking back what industry left behind. It is being carefully, sometimes paradoxically, invited to grow. The open chalk faces and delicate grasslands need tending. Even the butterfly bush, generous with flowers and unwelcome in certain habitats, is selectively removed so other life can make a home.

I gathered what had been cut, abandoned or overlooked. Flowers became tentative pigments; metal became a skin of time. I scanned surfaces, printed fragments, worked with fabric, water and three-dimensional painting, and listened for what would happen when one material entered the language of another.

Then the squares began to appear everywhere. In the factory, in the ground, in the mosaics we made. Perhaps the pattern had always been there. Perhaps my eyes had learned a new way of moving.

Nothing here settles into a single story. The archive holds the contradictions open: removal and renewal, extraction and care, the natural and the manufactured, what disappears and what insists on returning.

Landscape context: Natuurmonumenten, ENCI-area transformation and ecological management in the quarry.

02 / A way of working

From a trace
to a form.

I do not begin with the finished object. I begin with an encounter, and follow its consequences.

The sequence bends, breaks and starts again. Its purpose is to keep the question moving.

01

Inspiration

Something interrupts the ordinary: an unlikely texture, a square, a sound behind a wall.

Walking, noticing and learning to see the quarry differently.

02

Collect

I gather traces, not answers. Each fragment carries a place, a history, a possibility.

Butterfly bush, moss, rusted metal, electronic fragments, limestone dust, stones, ceramic balls, images and field recordings.

03

Experiment

What happens if a flower becomes colour, a surface becomes a print, or time becomes sound?

Inks, scent studies, scans, marbling, textiles, photography, painting, spatial arrangements and sound.

04

Build foundations

Temporary forms make the next question possible. A foundation need not be a conclusion.

Mosaic squares, installations, collective contributions, a soundscape and a growing archive.

∞

Research is the pulse, not the final chapter. It moves through the looking, gathering, making and returning. I document what changed, including what did not work, so that one experiment can begin another.

06 / Sonic landscape

Listening
across
deep time.

Before the quarry had walls, it had water. Beneath the limestone, I imagined ancient creatures moving in a darkness without machines. Their world fades into wings, voices and the restless breathing of the living terrain.

Then the rhythm changes: extraction, iron, weight. The present arrives almost quietly, full of footsteps and distance. A possible future swells until technology drowns out everything, then leaves a heartbeat and a single bird.

One continuous listening experience. Let it travel with you as you move through the images.

07 / What the work returned

Reflections
from DDW.

Three encounters changed the work: the landscape, the people beside me, and the people who came to see it.

Open each reflection to read my original DDW notes.

01Personal artistic insights+

My artistic research at ENCI expanded my ongoing exploration of interconnected systems into the landscape itself. I created a soundscape translating geological time, from the mosasaurs to a speculative future of “Chalk Paradise”, into a sensory experience. Through collecting materials, photography, scanning, printing, water marbling and three-dimensional painting, and embodied spatial awareness, I explored relationships between industrial remains and organic formations. Experimenting with butterfly bush inks, scents and fabric textures further revealed the creative potential of overlooked materials. Training myself to recognise square patterns throughout ENCI taught me how artistic observation can uncover connections between time, material, nature and human intervention.

02Learning through collaboration+

Collaborating within the Arts & Ecologies project showed me how individual experiments can become part of a larger interconnected system. I collected and contributed materials, created approximately ten mosaic squares, several of which were exhibited, and participated as an actress in the other group's short film. I particularly enjoyed the freedom to move between disciplines, exchanging ideas and discovering unexpected possibilities through experimentation. Each contribution became part of a collective exploration of ENCI's transforming landscape. This experience reminded me that artistic collaboration is not necessarily about creating something uniform, but about allowing different perspectives, materials and creative processes to coexist and influence one another. Natural evolution.

03The audience as part of the system+

What inspired me most during DDW were the conversations with visitors, especially while co-hosting with Saskia during the weekend. I discovered that audiences often experience artworks on a surface level until invited into the research and experimentation behind them. Most visitors expressed curiosity and surprise at the depth of our investigations, particularly considering our limited preparation time and second-year student status. Their responses strengthened my confidence and motivation to develop my practice further. Most importantly, I realised that sharing artistic processes creates meaningful connections between artist, audience and environment, transforming an exhibition from a presentation of objects into an exchange of personal perspectives and experiences.

08 / The questions that remain

What remains
in motion?

There is no final image of a place still changing. These are the questions I carry out of the quarry.

OBSERVATION

If I learn to see a square everywhere, what else might I have been overlooking?

TRANSFORMATION

Can something removed from an ecosystem find a second life without erasing why it was removed?

PARTICIPATION

What changes when the audience is no longer only looking, but touching the water, shifting the light, entering the work?
